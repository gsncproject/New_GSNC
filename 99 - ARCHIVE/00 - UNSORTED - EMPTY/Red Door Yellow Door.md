# Red Door, Yellow Door — The Game
>[!abstract] Overview 
>Red Door, Yellow Door (also known as Doors of the Mind, Black Door, White Door, or basically any colour combination) is a supposedly paranormal sleepover game involving guided visualisation, a trance-like state, and exploring rooms and doors inside one’s mind.

## 01 — What Is It?
- A game involving 2 people: a guide and a subject.
- The subject supposedly enters a trance and explores a mental environment consisting of rooms, corridors, and doors.
- The guide asks questions about what the subject sees and directs them through the environment.
- Different versions of the game have different names and rules, but the basic mechanics remain pretty similar.
## 02 — How to Play (According to the Article)
### Setup
1. The guide sits cross-legged on the floor with a pillow in their lap.
2. The subject lies down with their head resting in the guide’s lap.
3. The subject raises their hands into the air.
4. The guide massages the subject’s temples in circular motions.
5. Both repeatedly chant: “Red Door, Yellow Door, Any Other Colour Door.”
6. Once the subject supposedly enters the trance and begins seeing a room, they lower their arms.
7. The chanting stops.
### Entering the Mental Environment
- The guide starts asking questions about the room the subject is supposedly standing in.
- Questions can involve:  
	- What colour the doors are.
	- How the subject feels about the doors.
	- What is behind them.
	- Whether the subject should enter another room.
- The subject describes what they experience while the guide continues asking questions.
- Other people present are supposed to remain silent.
- The guide decides when to end the session.
## 03 — Supposed Rules & Dangers

| Rule                                                              | Supposed Meaning                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Avoid interacting with people encountered in the rooms            | They might supposedly be evil or deceptive.             |
| Leave rooms full of clocks immediately                            | Clocks supposedly have the ability to trap the subject. |
| Going up is safer than going down                                 | Downward movement is associated with danger.            |
| Light colours are supposedly safer than dark colours              | Darkness is associated with negative experiences.       |
| If trapped, attempt to wake up                                    | The subject supposedly risks becoming stuck forever.    |
| Dying in the game supposedly means dying IRL                      | A particularly extreme urban legend.                    |
| Encountering an uncomfortable man in a suit means ending the game | One of the recurring warnings.                          |

### Waking the Subject
- One version of the rules claims the guide should shake the subject awake if they cannot wake them.
- The article explicitly warns against this.
- Instead, it recommends stopping the questioning, speaking normally, saying the subject’s actual name, and giving them time to become fully alert.
- Roughly shaking someone who is disoriented can cause injury or panic.
## 04 — Psychological Explanation
>[!mind] Why Does It Work?  
>The article explains the experience primarily through trance induction, guided imagery, and suggestion, rather than paranormal explanations.

### Factors Involved
- **Repetition**: Chanting the same phrase repeatedly.
- **Physical contact**: Continuous temple massage.
- **Relaxation**: Lying down with eyes closed.
- **Darkness**: Reduced external stimulation.
- **Fatigue**: Often played late at night.
- **Suggestion**: The guide asks questions that assume the environment already exists.
### Leading Questions
There’s an important difference between:
- “Do you see a door?”
- “What colour is the door?”

The second question already assumes that a door exists. This can influence what the subject imagines.
### Guided Imagery
- The brain can produce vivid mental imagery in relaxed and suggestible states.
- Questions can influence the content of that imagery.
- The experience can feel like discovering something rather than consciously creating it.
- This doesn’t automatically mean the subject is lying or deliberately making things up.
## 05 — Reported Risks
The article distinguishes paranormal folklore from actual psychological risks.

Potential risks mentioned:
- Panic attacks.
- Sleep-paralysis-like sensations.
- Nightmares.
- Lingering anxiety.
- Distressing or overwhelming imagery.

The article recommends avoiding the game with people experiencing anxiety or panic attacks, stopping if someone becomes distressed, and bringing them out gently.
## 06 — Origins & History
- No reliable single origin has been established.
- Despite claims of ancient folklore or ancient ritual origins, the article says there is no evidence supporting those claims.
- It appears to be a modern sleepover game transmitted through word of mouth.
- Later versions spread through:  
	- Internet forums.
	- Creepypasta websites.
	- YouTube.
	- TikTok.
- Its different names are likely part of how the game evolved and spread between communities.
