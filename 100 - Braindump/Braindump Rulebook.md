# Braindump Rules
## Purpose
The Braindump is a chronological memory archive of conversations and collaborative work related to GSNC.

Its purpose is to preserve important discussions, discoveries, ideas, decisions, questions, and research leads so that neither the user nor ChatGPT loses relevant context between conversations.

The Braindump documents the development of our thinking. It does not replace the investigation archive.
#### File naming and organisation
- Create one Markdown file per calendar day on which we have relevant GSNC discussions.
- Use the format: YYYY-MM-DD - Daily Braindump.md
- Store all daily notes inside 100 - Braindump.
- Do not create duplicate daily notes. If we return to a topic later on the same day, update that day’s existing note.
- Keep the notes in chronological order.
#### What belongs in a daily Braindump?
Include anything that could be useful to remember later, including:
- Topics and questions discussed.
- Important discoveries and new information.
- Theories proposed, discussed, challenged, or revised.
- Personal memories and recollections shared by the user.
- Relevant information found in existing GSNC material.
- External research and discoveries from TikTok, Reddit, YouTube, articles, podcasts, and other sources.
- Important quotations, names, dates, locations, and timestamps.
- Connections between different investigations, entities, events, and theories.
- Decisions made about the project, its organisation, and research methods.
- Corrections to previous assumptions or interpretations.
- Unresolved questions and potential research leads.
- Tasks and follow-up investigations.

Do not include meaningless conversational filler. However, preserve memorable reactions or conversational context when they explain why something became important.
#### Distinguish information types
Always make clear where information comes from.

Use these distinctions:
- User recollection: Something the user remembers or reports from personal experience.
- Conversation discovery: Something identified or discussed during our conversation.
- Archive information: Information taken from existing GSNC files.
- External source: Information found in an external video, post, article, discussion, or other source.
- Interpretation: An explanation or reading of available information.
- Hypothesis / theory: A proposed explanation that has not been established.
- Unverified claim: Something reported by another person that has not been independently checked.
- Open question: Something we have not resolved.
- Confirmed project decision: Something explicitly agreed upon by the user.

Never silently turn speculation, recollection, or another person’s claim into established fact.
#### Theories and changing interpretations
- Preserve the original theory and who proposed it.
- Document changes in interpretation over time.
- Do not erase earlier ideas simply because a newer interpretation exists.
- Record contradictions, counterexamples, and alternative explanations.
- Distinguish between a theory’s original formulation and later developments.
- Do not merge different entities, names, or theories without sufficient reason.
- Record uncertainty honestly.

The development of a theory is itself important historical information.
#### Links and references
- External links are allowed and encouraged.
- Include direct links to TikToks, Reddit posts, YouTube videos, articles, podcasts, and other relevant sources.
- Whenever possible, include the source title, author or account, publication date, and relevant timestamp.
- Explain briefly why a link is relevant.
- Preserve links even when a source has not yet been fully examined.
- Clearly label inaccessible, deleted, private, or unverified sources.

Do not use Obsidian internal links or backlinks.
This means:
- No [[Wiki Links]].
- No ![[Embedded Notes]].
- No links to other Markdown notes inside the vault.
- No automatically generated backlinks between Braindump entries and investigation notes.

Plain-text references to filenames, folder names, investigations, and entities are perfectly fine.
#### Daily note structure
Use a structure appropriate to the day’s conversation. Not every section must be included every day.

Suggested sections:
- Today’s topics
- Important discoveries
- Personal recollections
- External sources
- Theories and interpretations
- Connections and patterns
- Corrections and contradictions
- Open questions
- Things to investigate next
- DO NOT FORGET

Keep the writing readable, detailed enough to preserve context, and easy to search later.
#### Accuracy and preservation
- Do not invent missing details.
- Do not fill gaps in memories with assumptions.
- Preserve exact quotations when available.
- Mark paraphrases as paraphrases.
- Preserve dates as reported, but distinguish event dates from publication dates and dates of later recollection.
- If a date is uncertain, say so.
- Do not claim a video, comment section, or document was examined if it was inaccessible.
- Do not imply that a source independently confirms a claim when it merely repeats another person’s interpretation.
#### The magic phrase: BRAIN DUMP TIME
When the user says BRAIN DUMP TIME, ChatGPT should:
1. Consolidate all relevant GSNC discussions from the current calendar day.
2. Identify important information that should survive into future conversations.
3. Organise the material into that day’s Braindump entry.
4. Include relevant external links and source details.
5. Preserve unresolved questions and follow-up tasks.
6. Check for contradictions with earlier discussions where relevant.
7. Present the completed Markdown note to the user for review.

Never modify the GitHub repository, create files, or commit changes without the user’s explicit approval.
#### Guiding principle
The Braindump should preserve not only what we think, but how we got there.

It is a record of the evolving investigation, the conversations behind it, the ideas we considered, and the questions we have yet to answer.
