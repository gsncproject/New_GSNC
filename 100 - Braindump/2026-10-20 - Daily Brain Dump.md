Part IV — Continuing the Saved TikTok Review

Current goal

- Source day: 2026-10-20. I want to go through my saved TikToks and document the ones that contain material worth keeping for GSNC research.
- The aim is to preserve useful observations, claims, comments, and research leads before they are forgotten.
- Not every saved TikTok needs to become a major investigation. Some may only deserve a short note; others may be irrelevant.

Practical workflow

1. Open a saved TikTok and identify what it is actually about.
2. Preserve the direct video link.
3. Identify the creator and record the original upload date if it can be verified.
4. Use the original upload date for the filename when known. Record the date the note was documented separately when useful.
5. Summarize what the video shows or says.
6. Separate the creator’s claims from comments and my own interpretation.
7. Preserve only the comments that provide useful observations, specific claims, or leads.
8. Decide whether the video is worth keeping for research, might be useful later, or is not relevant.
9. Check for an existing note before creating another one about the same video.
10. Leave uncertain dates and details explicitly uncertain rather than filling in gaps with guesses.

Filename and location conventions

- Folder: 01 - Content/99 - Video Notes
- Suggested filename pattern: YYYY-MM-DD - TikTok - @creator - Short Topic.md
- The date should represent the original upload date when verifiable, not merely the date I happened to document the video.
- If the original upload date cannot be established, mark it as unknown or approximate instead of inventing a precise date.
- The note itself can record the date it was documented.
- Video notes should contain ordinary external URLs where useful, not Obsidian wiki links or embedded-note links.
- These notes are an initial collection and triage point. A TikTok’s claim should not automatically become a conclusion in a larger research document.

Triage principle

- Save for research: Specific observations, source material, useful comments, potentially verifiable claims, or meaningful connections to existing research.
- Maybe later: Interesting but currently vague, repetitive, or lacking enough information to investigate.
- Not relevant: Little research value, no identifiable lead, or material already adequately documented elsewhere.

These categories are workflow decisions, not judgments about whether a creator or commenter is telling the truth.
