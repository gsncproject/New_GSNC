Part IV — Mimicry as a Research Question

The user’s position

The user considers mimicry a particularly interesting but complicated topic. They recalled searching online and finding that a “mimic” is not an established real paranormal entity.

The conversation distinguished between using mimicry to describe reported behaviour and treating Mimic as a proven supernatural entity category.

The user clarified that, in their working understanding, the suspected pattern involves both resemblance and broader imitation: figures that reportedly look like Sam or Colby and may also be presenting themselves as, or imitating, those people more generally.

This is a research hypothesis, not a verified explanation of the cases.

Working distinctions

For future GSNC documentation, the following terms should remain distinct.

Resemblance: A reported figure looks like a particular person.

Imitation: A reported figure copies some aspect of a person’s appearance, voice, mannerisms, or behaviour.

Impersonation: A figure is reported to present itself as a particular person or identity, potentially misleading others about who it is.

Mimic as an entity category: A claim that a distinct kind of supernatural being exists whose defining characteristic is imitation.

The first three can describe reports without establishing the fourth.

Application to the current cases

- Sams 1–4: Reportedly resemble Sam Golbach but differ in appearance and alleged disposition.
- Literally Fucking Colby: Reportedly resembles Colby Brock.
- Lucy: Appears in older GSNC notes about the possibility of impersonating other identities.
- Sallie, Emily, and Susie: Appear in the historical theory that one underlying figure might present itself through multiple identities.

These cases can be compared under the question of apparent identity without assuming that all of them share the same explanation.

Two competing models still under consideration

Model 1 — One underlying identity, multiple presentations

Several apparent identities could be interpreted as presentations of one underlying figure. This resembles the structure of the old Lucy–Sallie–Emily–Susie theory.

Model 2 — Separate figures imitating the same person

Multiple separate figures could reportedly resemble or imitate Sam or Colby.

Neither model has been established. The current material does not justify choosing between them.

Open questions

- What specific observations distinguish resemblance from imitation in each case?
- Is there evidence of a figure explicitly presenting itself as another identity?
- Are the reported identities understood as one underlying figure or several separate figures?
- Does the same pattern recur across cases, or are superficially similar descriptions being grouped together?
- What ordinary explanations and alternative interpretations should be considered for each report?
